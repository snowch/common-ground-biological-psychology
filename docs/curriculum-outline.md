# Curriculum and evidence map

## 1. A brain explanation needs a comparison

Distinguish neural association, intervention and reverse inference in a claim about behaviour.

- Biological psychology connects levels.
- Methods reveal different things.
- Do not read a mind from a hotspot.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: The brain and spinal cord](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/3-4-the-brain-and-spinal-cord); [POLDRACK (2006): Can cognitive processes be inferred from neuroimaging data?](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2005.12.004)

## 2. A signal is a process, not a spark of personality

Explain an action potential and distinguish electrical propagation from synaptic transmission.

- Cells maintain an electrical difference.
- An action potential regenerates.
- The connection changes the next cell.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Cells of the nervous system](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/3-2-cells-of-the-nervous-system); [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Parts of the nervous system](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/3-3-parts-of-the-nervous-system)

## 3. Networks work together

Explain a brain function using interacting networks rather than a single-region personality label.

- Anatomy gives landmarks, not isolated minds.
- Lateralization is not a personality test.
- Damage affects connected systems.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: The brain and spinal cord](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/3-4-the-brain-and-spinal-cord); [POLDRACK (2006): Can cognitive processes be inferred from neuroimaging data?](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2005.12.004)

## 4. Sensing is active biological work

Distinguish physical stimulation, transduction and perceptual interpretation.

- Energy must become a neural signal.
- Sensory systems adapt.
- Perception uses more than receptors.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Sensation versus perception](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/5-1-sensation-versus-perception); [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Hearing](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/5-4-hearing)

## 5. The body helps organize a response

Explain autonomic and hormonal responses without treating one physiological signal as a unique emotion.

- Regulation involves connected systems.
- Hormones signal through receptors.
- Arousal does not name the feeling.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Parts of the nervous system](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/3-3-parts-of-the-nervous-system); [OpenStax Psychology 2e: The endocrine system](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/3-5-the-endocrine-system)

## 6. Stress is a response in context

Differentiate an acute adaptive response from claims about chronic stress and allostatic load.

- Acute responses can support action.
- Regulation has more than one pathway.
- Repeated strain has social conditions.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: What is stress](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/14-1-what-is-stress); [McEwen (1998): Protective and Damaging Effects of Stress Mediators](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199801153380307)

## 7. Sleep has timing and structure

Distinguish circadian timing, sleep pressure and sleep stages in a practical sleep claim.

- Two processes shape sleep timing.
- Sleep is not one uniform state.
- A tracker is an estimate.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Sleep and why we sleep](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/4-2-sleep-and-why-we-sleep); [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Stages of sleep](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/4-3-stages-of-sleep)

## 8. Genes act in developing systems

Interpret heritability without treating a genetic association as individual destiny.

- Genes are part of a process.
- Heritability describes variation in a population.
- Prediction and explanation differ.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Human genetics](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/3-1-human-genetics); [Polderman, Benyamin, de Leeuw (2015): Meta-analysis of the heritability of human traits based on fifty years of twin studies](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng.3285)

## 9. Experience changes systems, within limits

Distinguish task-specific plasticity from a claim of general brain improvement.

- Plasticity is change, not a promise.
- Practice can improve a specific task.
- Rehabilitation is individualized.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: The brain and spinal cord](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/3-4-the-brain-and-spinal-cord); [Draganski, Gaser, Busch (2004): Changes in grey matter induced by training](https://doi.org/10.1038/427311a)

## 10. Build a biological explanation carefully

Combine neural, psychological and contextual evidence in a bounded practical recommendation.

- Connect levels without skipping steps.
- Evaluate the intervention claim separately.
- Keep choices proportionate.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Introduction](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/3-introduction); [POLDRACK (2006): Can cognitive processes be inferred from neuroimaging data?](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2005.12.004); [Draganski, Gaser, Busch (2004): Changes in grey matter induced by training](https://doi.org/10.1038/427311a)
