# Learning design

Audience: interested adult beginners who want to interpret human behaviour using evidence. The gap is between attaching an intuitive label and explaining a case with accurate concepts, alternatives and limits. Delivery is a standalone, accessible, learner-paced GitHub Pages course, not an LMS or certification.

The established café visual identity uses Libre Baskerville headings, Arimo body text, pale blue backgrounds, navy text, teal interactions and restrained chapter accents. Reading and controls precede the supporting figure in the stacked layout. The primary learning path remains clear when research disclosures stay closed.

Each chapter has a first decision, three separate narrated explanations, a visible evidence check, a new written transfer case, unaided retrieval and low-stakes field practice. The final case integrates chapters. Page visits indicate navigation, not mastery. No learner pilot or independent academic review has been conducted.

## 1. A brain explanation needs a comparison

Objective: Distinguish neural association, intervention and reverse inference in a claim about behaviour.

Initial decision: What is missing from the fear claim?

Transfer: A scalp recording differs while participants hear frequent and unexpected sounds. Which conclusion is best supported?

Field application: Inspect a public brain-image headline. Name its measure, comparison and one alternative interpretation.

## 2. A signal is a process, not a spark of personality

Objective: Explain an action potential and distinguish electrical propagation from synaptic transmission.

Initial decision: Which correction improves the wire metaphor?

Transfer: The same neurotransmitter is found in pathways supporting different functions. What should the explanation include?

Field application: Explain a neural signal in your own words using three stages. Identify where the wire metaphor stops being useful.

## 3. Networks work together

Objective: Explain a brain function using interacting networks rather than a single-region personality label.

Initial decision: What best corrects Leo’s claim?

Transfer: After a lesion, a person struggles with a particular task, but several connected pathways were affected. What inference is proportionate?

Field application: Rewrite a left-brain/right-brain headline as a testable statement about a specified task and measure.

## 4. Sensing is active biological work

Objective: Distinguish physical stimulation, transduction and perceptual interpretation.

Initial decision: What is missing from Leo’s explanation?

Transfer: A visitor follows instructions more accurately when a spoken announcement also appears as clear text. Which conclusion is reasonable?

Field application: Inspect your own low-stakes listening environment. Identify a signal, a source of competition and an accessibility support.

## 5. The body helps organize a response

Objective: Explain autonomic and hormonal responses without treating one physiological signal as a unique emotion.

Initial decision: What can the pulse establish by itself?

Transfer: A social-media post interprets one hormone measurement as a complete measure of chronic stress. What additional information matters?

Field application: For a fictional presentation scene, list one bodily observation, two possible explanations and a respectful question. Do not monitor or diagnose another person.

## 6. Stress is a response in context

Objective: Differentiate an acute adaptive response from claims about chronic stress and allostatic load.

Initial decision: How should the headline be qualified?

Transfer: A volunteer reports repeated overload because staffing and break arrangements have changed. What response fits the situation best?

Field application: Use a fictional recurring workload problem. Separate demand, response, recovery opportunity and a change in the environment.

## 7. Sleep has timing and structure

Objective: Distinguish circadian timing, sleep pressure and sleep stages in a practical sleep claim.

Initial decision: What is missing from Leo’s instruction?

Transfer: A wrist device labels several minutes as a specific sleep stage using movement and pulse data. Which conclusion fits the evidence?

Field application: Evaluate a public sleep-device claim using its measured signals and intended use. Do not change your sleep duration to run an experiment.

## 8. Genes act in developing systems

Objective: Interpret heritability without treating a genetic association as individual destiny.

Initial decision: What does a heritability estimate describe?

Transfer: A company offers a DNA-based score to decide who should lead a volunteer team. What should be checked before using the score?

Field application: Rewrite a public genes-for-behaviour headline with the population, measured outcome and uncertainty made explicit.

## 9. Experience changes systems, within limits

Objective: Distinguish task-specific plasticity from a claim of general brain improvement.

Initial decision: What does Sam’s improvement support most directly?

Transfer: A training programme claims better everyday reasoning after practice on one game. What would best test the broader claim?

Field application: Choose a skill you practise. State one trained outcome and one untrained outcome needed to support a broader claim.

## 10. Build a biological explanation carefully

Objective: Combine neural, psychological and contextual evidence in a bounded practical recommendation.

Initial decision: What should the café ask first?

Transfer: A small uncontrolled study finds a brain-signal difference after a workshop but measures no daily-life benefit. Which conclusion is proportionate?

Field application: Write a brief review of a fictional brain-based product: claimed outcome, actual measure, missing comparison and a proportionate conclusion.
