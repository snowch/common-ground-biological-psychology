An association between a brain signal and an event is a starting point. It does not uniquely identify what one person thought.

c01s03b
Electroencephalography: recording electrical activity at the scalp. Functional imaging: estimating activity-related physiological changes. Lesion evidence: studying consequences of tissue damage.

Scalp electrical recordings have useful temporal resolution but limited source localization. Functional magnetic resonance imaging measures blood-oxygen-related changes rather than thoughts directly.

Lesions and carefully designed interventions can add causal evidence, but damage or stimulation can affect connected systems. Each method has practical and inferential limits.

c01s04
Reverse inference: inferring a process from associated brain activity. Converging evidence: support across suitable methods.

If fear sometimes involves a region, activity there does not prove fear. The same region may participate in attention, learning and other tasks.

At many synapses, an arriving signal triggers chemical release. A transmitter binds receptors that affect the receiving cell. Other synapses transmit electrically.

The effect depends on receptors, cell state and circuitry. A transmitter is not inherently a single emotion, and more of it does not imply more happiness.
